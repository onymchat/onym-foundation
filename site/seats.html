THE SEAT MAP

Four layers, seventeen open roles.

The person in the middle. Distance is intimacy: protocol components touch the daily route; market services attribute trust; applications serve particular domains; legal institutions remain governed by law. The Foundation sits outside the route, funding common infrastructure. Registering interest is a hello, not a contract.

Protocol kernel

Identity, interface, message, blob, and notary boundaries. Replacement requires wire compatibility and, for shared state, governed migration.

Service market

Recovery and its custody profiles, device backup, Discovery, audit, and arbitration. Signatures attribute claims; they do not manufacture competence or truth, and holding a copy of something is never the same as holding the person.

Application profiles

Charity, banking, distribution, and acquisition. Portability does not override regulation, custody, or domain liability.

Organizational governance

Sponsorship and recruitment. Signed records support accountability but do not replace legal appointment, labor law, or fiduciary duty.

Moderation authority

live on iOS · Android draft

Decides abuse reports users consented to at onboarding — notice, response window, reasoned verdicts, declared durations, appeal. Interfaces execute; the authority never touches keys, and sees only the single messages a recipient chose to disclose — never a mailbox, never anything undisclosed.

Earns: interface fees per report adjudicated — never per ban or per case opened — and foundation grants · Fits: trust-and-safety firms, hotlines, civil-society organizations

An authority that accepts reported photographs as evidence must say so in its manifest, publish the periods after which it deletes them, and — for any class it reports to the state — publish the preservation and referral terms too, or refuse media for that class. The Apple path runs in production today — an authority opening cases, serving notice, and signing verdicts, and the DeviceCheck backend that executes them — both from the Rust reference implementation and both currently operated by Onym as the replaceable default. The contract means those halves to sit with different operators, so one operator holding both is a concentration to replace, not a feature. The Android device-recall path is draft and undeployed, and nothing here is audited. This seat costs every user deniability — see what this seat costs everyone. Device backup

draft spec · no profile yet

Keeps a sealed copy of one person's messages and attachments so a lost device is not a lost history — and pins, before the first byte, how long it keeps them, when it erases them, whose law applies, and how it hands them back.

Earns: storage plans and prepaid quotas priced by bytes and retention — never by reading, indexing, or analyzing what it holds · Fits: hosting providers, co-ops, backup specialists, self-hosters

The contract holds no key and offers no reset path: a restore proves possession of the person's own key, so a lost key means an unreadable archive and no operator recourse. CONSEQUENCE

What the moderation seat costs everyone

A seat that only some users choose, paid for by all of them.

The evidence rule

An authority decides a report by verifying the accused's own signature over the disclosed content. That forces the property in both directions: if a recipient could manufacture the signature, anyone could be framed for words they never wrote; if nobody could produce it, no report could be checked and abuse would be unanswerable.

What it costs

Every message an interface sends must therefore carry a transferable proof of authorship — one the recipient can hand to somebody who was never in the conversation. Deniability goes, for every user, whether or not they ever consent to an authority and whether or not anyone is ever reported.

Why it is written here

This is a governance fact, not an implementation detail. Adopting a moderation seat changes a property of the messenger itself, so the cost belongs in the public record beside the seat that imposes it rather than in a client's release notes.

The bound is narrow and worth stating exactly: an identity is a public key, not a name, and the proof says only that the holder of that key wrote those words. It becomes a proof about a person when the key is linked to one by information that lives outside the protocol. The signature also sits inside the encryption — it changes who can prove authorship of a message they already hold, never who can read one. Interfaces disclose this to users directly; see onym.app/privacy.

CONSEQUENCE

What durable copies cost everyone

One new seat and one existing seat's profiles both sell the same thing: a copy that persists exactly when the person is absent. Durability and secrecy pull against each other, so the terms are the product — which is why they are published here beside the capability.

What an operator must settle before it holds anything

Pinned in advance, binding forward

Retention period, the deadline for honouring an erase request and what erasure provably does not cover, jurisdictions and sub-processors, the export format the person can walk away with, the price, what happens when payment stops, and how a breach is disclosed. New terms govern new snapshots; a policy that can be restated after the data has arrived is leverage over someone's history, not a service.

Sealed, or it is not a backup

Ciphertext only; keys stay on the device; no server-side indexing or deduplication by content; restore proves possession of the person's own key rather than an account password an operator can reset. The honest cost of that rule is stated too: a lost key means an unreadable archive and no operator recourse, because every mechanism that would provide recourse would let the operator read what it stores.

A single holder is custody, and must say so

Seed custody is not a seat of its own — it is how the recovery trustee's profiles differ. The threshold profile splits material so no one holder can use it; the cloud profile is a declared 1-of-1 custodian whose operator can technically recover what it stores. Both are legitimate; only one is sealed, and a person choosing a single holder is owed that distinction before enrolment rather than after a subpoena.

And what it still costs, once all of that is honoured

Backups outlive the sender's expectation

Message carriage and media storage can promise short retention; a recipient's backup quietly extends it for both sides of every conversation. Deletion stops at the edge of other people's archives, and the sender is never asked. Anyone reading a retention promise should read it as covering the route, not the copies people keep at the ends of it.

A single holder concentrates compulsion

A custodial recovery holder can be ordered, breached, or pressured to produce what it holds, in a jurisdiction the person chose once and may not revisit. The exposure exists from enrolment, not from use, and the identity's safety then depends on a stranger's operational security as well as the holder's. A threshold set spreads that; it does not remove it, since any threshold can collude.

Why it is written here

No operator can be trusted to disclose the tension between durability and secrecy in its own pricing page. The Foundation would fund grants and audits for these seats once their contracts merge, so the cost belongs in its public record beside the capability — the same rule applied to the moderation seat above.

The bounds, stated exactly: none of this changes what a courier can read or what encryption protects in transit — it changes how long a copy exists and who else can be compelled to produce it. A backup restores history, never an identity: seed material, recovery artifacts, and trustee shares are ineligible for a snapshot, which is what the recovery trustee seat exists for. What the Foundation is asking upstream for is the profile that is missing — a sealed single-holder custody profile whose operator cannot unwrap what it holds, beside the custodial one that already exists.
